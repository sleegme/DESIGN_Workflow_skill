## Ground the direction in the subject

Define the subject, audience, and single primary job before choosing a visual
direction. Derive visual language from the subject's real materials, vocabulary,
artifacts, environments, and constraints.

Avoid a direction that could be pasted unchanged onto another product in the same
category.

## Build a compact direction

Specify:

- **Atmosphere:** mood, density, rhythm, and degree of restraint.
- **Color:** a small functional palette with named roles and precise values when
  the medium supports them.
- **Typography:** deliberate roles for display, body, and utility/data text.
- **Layout:** one organizing idea that expresses the content, not decoration.
- **Signature:** one memorable element that is specific to the brief.
- **Motion or sequence:** only where it improves comprehension or character.

Spend boldness in one place. Keep the surrounding system disciplined.

## Two-pass process

1. Draft the direction and a compact token/behavior plan.
2. Critique it against the brief:
   - Is any choice a generic default?
   - Does the signature belong to this subject?
   - Is the complexity appropriate?
   - Does the copy sound like the product rather than generic promotion?
3. Revise weak choices before implementation.
4. Build from the revised plan rather than improvising new motifs midstream.
5. Inspect the result in context and remove one unnecessary flourish.

## Content and interaction

- Write from the end user's perspective.
- Use plain, consistent action labels.
- Treat empty, loading, and failure states as functional design.
- Preserve accessible focus, contrast, target sizes, and reduced-motion behavior.
- Do not fabricate statistics, claims, testimonials, or data.
