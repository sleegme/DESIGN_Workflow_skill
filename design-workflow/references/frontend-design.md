## Ground the direction

Define the subject, audience, primary job, constraints, and real content before
choosing a visual direction. Derive the system from the subject's materials,
vocabulary, environment, and use conditions.

Avoid a direction that could be pasted unchanged onto an unrelated product in
the same category.

## Define a compact system

Specify:

- **Atmosphere:** mood, density, rhythm, and restraint.
- **Color:** a small functional palette with named roles and precise values.
- **Typography:** deliberate display, body, utility, and data roles.
- **Layout:** one organizing idea that expresses the content.
- **Signature:** one memorable subject-specific element.
- **Motion or sequence:** only where it improves comprehension or character.
- **Accessibility:** contrast, focus, target sizes, reading order, and reduced
  motion.

Spend boldness in one place and keep the surrounding system disciplined.

## Create route

1. Confirm that no meaningful design dimension is being silently discarded.
2. Draft a direction and compact token or behavior plan.
3. Critique generic choices and revise them before implementation.
4. Execute through the appropriate medium workflow.
5. Inspect in context and remove one unnecessary flourish.

## Redesign route

Before generating alternatives, write a replacement map:

- dimensions explicitly authorized for replacement
- dimensions that must remain recognizable
- content, behavior, legal, accessibility, and platform constraints
- migration or compatibility requirements

Do not use redesign permission for one dimension to replace all other dimensions.

## Content integrity

Write from the end user's perspective. Use consistent action labels and real
content where available. Never fabricate statistics, testimonials, claims, or
product capabilities.
